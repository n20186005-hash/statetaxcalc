Tax Planning Insights

How Brackets Work

The US has a progressive tax system. You don’t pay the same rate on every dollar. It's like climbing stairs; you only pay a higher "toll" (rate) on the income that exceeds that specific step.

Strategies to Lower Taxes

  • Retirement: Contributing to a 401(k) or Traditional IRA reduces your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. Try the slider above!
  • HSA: If you have a high-deductible plan, HSA contributions directly reduce taxable income.

How to determine your tax bracket?

First look at the tax rates for your filing status. Calculate taxable income by adding all income and subtracting deductions. The top rate your income falls into is not the rate you pay on *all* income—only the portion in that specific bracket.

What is your federal marginal tax rate?

Federal marginal tax rate is the rate owed on your highest dollar earned. For example, if you fall into the 24% bracket, your marginal rate is 24%. You owe 24 cents on every additional dollar earned.

What is your federal effective tax rate?

Your effective tax rate is the total percentage of income paid in federal income tax (Total Tax / Total Income). It is essentially the average of the different rates paid across all brackets.

Potential ways to get into a lower tax bracket

  • Maximize Deductions: Claim all eligible deductions.
  • Bunching Deductions: Concentrate charitable donations.
  • Retirement Savings: Contribute to 401(k) or Traditional IRA.
  • Health Savings Account: HSA contributions reduce taxable income.
